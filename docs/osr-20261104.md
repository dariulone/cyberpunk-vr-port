# OSR -- the port's own reconstruction of the other eye under AFW (2026-11-04)

OFXR-Bridge (github.com/tig3rmast3r/OFXR-Bridge) ships "OSR", OFXR Stereo Reconstruction, as its default over
PureDark's AFW for one-eye-per-frame games ("less ghosting and a lower GPU cost than AFW"). Its code is GPL-3.0 and
the port is MIT, so nothing of it is used: `src/Render/Osr.cpp` is written from the technique its
docs/OSR_FULL_WARP.md describes.

## What it does (four compute passes on the capture list, cs_5_0, compiled at first use)

1. **Copy.** The drawn eye's colour and depth go into that eye's history: they are the source now and the made
   eye's previous frame in the next frame (the eyes take turns).
2. **Splat.** Every depth texel of the drawn eye is unprojected through its camera (reversed depth, the eye's OpenXR
   window) and projected into the made eye's camera; each cell of the made eye's depth grid keeps the nearest that
   lands on it (`InterlockedMax`, 2x2 footprint). The made eye's previous frame is splatted the same way into a second
   grid, through the camera it was drawn with.
3. **Resolve**, per pixel of the made eye: its cell's splatted depth (else the farthest, then the nearest of the 3x3)
   is traced back into the drawn eye; the colour is taken where the depth there agrees (3% of the distance; sky with
   sky). Else the same against the previous frame. What neither accounts for is a hole.
4. **Fill.** A hole is background only the made eye sees: along the row the farther bounding pixel is the background
   and the hole takes it mirrored across that edge (the edge pixel where the mirror lands on something nearer); a
   row that cannot close tries the column.

The depth is the jittered DLSS input; the colour is resolved. Each depth texel is read at its centre minus the frame's
jitter (measured best of +, -, none: see below).

## Switch

Two switches of their own: `xr_osr=1` (*Stereo view -> OSR*) and `xr_afw=1` (*Stereo view -> AFW (PureDark frame
warp)*); the menu keeps one at a time, both set in the ini means OSR. On or off needs a restart; between the two in a
launch that takes turns, at once. `CyberpunkVR_Afw` holds 0 off, 1 AFW, 2 OSR. Without the plugin installed AFW falls
back to OSR. (`xr_afw_reconstruction` is gone.) Live tuning: `CyberpunkVR_AfwTune[6]` reconstruction (1 OSR, 0
plugin), `[7]` jitter (0 minus, 1 plus, 2 none), `[0]` 1 = no history, `[3]` debug tint (history green, fill: magenta
closed run, cyan lone bound, yellow whole-image search); `CyberpunkVR_OsrTune[0]` depth tolerance x1000, `[1]` fill
search reach (px, 384), `[2]` near limit for what the drawn eye cannot confirm (cm, 100), `[3]` edge distance (px x100,
75; 0 = off); stats `CyberpunkVR_OsrStats` (made, failed, with history).

## Hands fix (2026-11-04, second pass)

Reported in the headset: a second pair of hands with an outline, a piece of forearm near the elbow, and OSR shimmering
everywhere while AFW only did around the hands. Causes and fixes (`tools/afw_tests/osrhands.py` moves both hands
sideways in front of the face and shoots both eyes per setting, debug tint on and off; `osrflicker.py`, `osrmotion.py`):

- **Second pair of hands.** The made eye's previous frame filled the holes the hands open; it is reprojected by the
  camera only, so the hands came back at their old place. Now every previous-frame surface is checked against this
  frame's drawn eye (`StillThere`): where the drawn eye sees past its place it moved and is dropped. Hidden or out of
  the drawn eye's view, a surface nearer than 1 m is taken for the player's own body (hands hide their own old place
  by the elbow) and dropped too. Still scenery passes, so the floor behind the hands comes from the previous frame.
- **Outline.** A drawn-eye sample beside a nearer surface carries that surface's anti-aliased colour; taken as the
  background it drew a thin copy of the near outline where the parallax had moved it. Samples with a nearer surface
  within 1.5 depth texels whose parallax differs by more than 0.75 px are left to the previous frame / fill (the
  nearest of the 4x4 is made once per texel in the Clear pass).
- **Piece of arm in the fill.** Holes beside the forearm are 150-250 px wide; the fill searched 96 px, found only the
  arm's side and smeared its colour, or fell back to the drawn eye's pixel at the same place (the arm). The search
  now reaches 384 px (strides of 4 after 16), takes the farthest lone bound when a run does not close, then the
  farthest bound across the image.
- **Shimmer everywhere** (an intermediate version): the previous-frame check failed at every silhouette (the
  footprint takes in the background), so every edge of the scene went to the mirror fill and changed each frame. It
  now fails only where all four texels are farther.

Results: hands clean in both eyes in the sway; fill 0.01-0.02 % of the made eye with the head still or turning
+-25 deg; still-scene change between shots (pixels changing > 24 grey levels, full resolution) OSR 0.49 % vs PureDark
0.80 %. GPU `CaptureSecond` 0.61 ms (was 0.44; PureDark 1.59). Left: a never-seen corner by the elbow at the image
edge is mirror-filled (tile seams can mirror there).

## The same checks as AFW (ManualSave-491, H10 street, simulator, RTX 5070 Ti)

Head still: with a still scene every real frame of an eye is the same picture, so each eye's stills differ from the
eye's per-pixel median only where a made frame differs from a real one (12 stills a configuration,
`tools/afw_tests/afwq.py`):

| reconstruction            | left worst / mean dB | right worst / mean dB |
|---------------------------|---------------------:|----------------------:|
| PureDark AFW (Combined)   |        33.84 / 36.28 |         33.33 / 36.24 |
| OSR, jitter subtracted    |    **35.71 / 37.88** |     **35.78 / 37.76** |
| OSR, jitter added         |        33.12 / 37.15 |         32.84 / 37.32 |
| OSR, no jitter            |        31.10 / 32.42 |         31.12 / 32.36 |
| OSR, no history           |        31.63 / 33.67 |         31.28 / 33.59 |

GPU time of the second eye (`CaptureSecond`, GPU stage profile): OSR 0.44 ms, PureDark AFW 1.59 ms. Head sweep
+-35 deg (8 frames, `afwab.py`): OSR clean on the arms (no doubling), as AFW Combined. Every frame made, 0 fallbacks,
eyes alternating, ~65-68 FPS at the CPU ceiling either way.

## Limits

- Object motion is not extrapolated into the previous frame (camera-only reprojection): a moving object's previous
  frame is dropped where this frame's drawn eye shows it gone (StillThere).
- The fill mirrors background; it removes streaks, it does not invent detail.
- Screen-space glow over a near surface is warped at the depth behind it, as with AFW.
